Referee remote + scoreboard

A handheld referee scoring and game-data system designed to make community basketball faster, more accurate and easier to run.

The project moves scoring input from inexperienced courtside volunteers to the referee, removing the communication step that causes delays, missed calls and incorrect player data.

01 · Need

The problem is not only the scoreboard. It is the communication chain.

In community games, parents are commonly rostered to score from a courtside table. They may need to relearn the system each week while also interpreting fast referee hand signals through players and other visual obstructions. That creates delays, incorrect scores or player numbers, and time spent stopping the game to fix errors.

Players

Lose playing time and can have the outcome or recorded statistics affected by scoring errors.

Parents

Must concentrate on an unfamiliar system instead of watching the game, while interpreting calls they may not fully understand.

Referees

Need to clarify signals, monitor the scoreboard and correct mistakes, adding another interruption to the game.

Basketball centres

Deal with training, administration, disputes and customer frustration when the recorded game does not match what happened on court.

02 · Solution

Put the scoring controls in the referee’s hand.

CourtSync lets the referee identify the team and player, record points or a foul, and keep the game information with the call itself. The goal is to reduce errors while preserving the information needed for the scoreboard and later game review.

03 · Prototype

The design evolved from a scoring concept into a complete embedded system.

Later development in the folio moves beyond the early concept into custom electronics, a purpose-built enclosure, a higher-resolution display, reliable Bluetooth transfer and persistent game storage.

01ESP32-S3 + custom PCB

KiCad schematic and PCB development brought the controller, button inputs, display connections and supporting electronics into a compact board.

021.54″ 240×240 ST7789

The firmware was reworked for a square ST7789 panel so the scoreboard and player information use the larger display area effectively.

0316-button direct-GPIO keypad

The final PCB revision removed four buttons and abandoned the earlier button matrix, wiring each remaining key directly to an ESP32-S3 GPIO for simpler, more predictable input.

04Bluetooth LE sync

Game exports use acknowledged BLE indications to prioritise reliable packet delivery over raw transfer speed.

05Persistent game history

Saved events are protected with bounds checking and the game clock is re-anchored after a reboot so event ordering remains meaningful.

06Local data viewer

A self-contained HTML viewer turns exported records into a scoreboard, per-player totals and event history without needing a server.

04 · Design criteria

Success depends on speed, clarity and physical reliability—not just whether the electronics turn on.

One-handed operation

The remote must be easy to reach and operate with enough spacing to avoid accidental inputs.

Fast response

Inputs should appear effectively in real time so the new method does not create a different kind of delay.

Accuracy & recovery

Clear feedback, cancellation and review reduce the risk of undetected input mistakes.

Durability

The enclosure must protect the electronics from drops, bumps and the conditions of a live basketball court.

Battery life

The design target is to remain usable across an entire evening of games rather than needing mid-session charging.

Low carried weight

The remote should not interfere with referee movement or become tiring over repeated games.

05 · Development

Iteration moved from proof-of-concept electronics to firmware reliability and data usability.

Research

Community scoring problem identified

Player and parent research established that time loss, unclear referee signals and incorrect inputs were recurring problems worth solving.

Electronics

Custom ESP32 PCB developed in KiCad

Schematic and board-layout experiments moved the design away from breadboard prototyping toward a compact purpose-built controller.

Mechanical design

PCB and housing refined together in SolidWorks

The enclosure was shortened and reshaped around the board, keypad, display, mounting points and connector clearances.

Interface

Display upgraded to a 240×240 ST7789

The UI was rebalanced for the larger square display instead of simply leaving unused pixels around the original layout.

Firmware

BLE transfer, clock persistence and storage hardened

Testing addressed dropped packets, sync-state faults, reboot timestamps, storage bounds and cross-task state access.

Data

Standalone game viewer created

The export pipeline now has a local visual endpoint for checking scores, player totals and the event sequence after a game.

Target market

Community basketball centres and casual hosted competitions, where the scoring role is commonly handled by volunteers and the benefit reaches referees, players, parents and spectators rather than one narrow age or gender group.
